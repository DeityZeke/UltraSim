# UltraSim Magic, Elements, Reification, Traditions, and Spell Semantics

---

## 1. Core Metaphysical Model

> **Scope note:** The Determination → Conjuration → Reification model in this document primarily defines **Wizardry / Arcane / Elemental Magic**. It is not intended to explain every supernatural tradition in the world. Other traditions may operate on fundamentally different principles and only share terminology where it is actually useful.

Magic is best treated as a **reality emulator**.

A magical construct can imitate the shape, behavior, force, appearance, and other relevant properties of something without initially reproducing every physical detail of that thing. A conjured flame can look and behave like fire without being ordinary combustion. A conjured water ball can behave like water without initially requiring several kilograms of persistent real matter.

The current core vocabulary is:

- **Conjuration** — the magical construct or imitation.
- **Determination** — what that construct is intended and paid to be capable of doing.
- **Reification** — the portion of that intended magical consequence which successfully becomes physically real when necessary.
- **Materialization** — deliberate full or near-full reification intended to create persistent mundane matter or energy.
- **Persistence** — the result continues to exist without the magical construct sustaining it.

A useful general rule is:

> **Magic pays up front for what the construct is determined to be capable of doing.**

Mana is not extracted later when the spell finally hits. The cast cost is based on the caster's assumption of what the spell should accomplish **if it succeeds**: magnitude, complexity, duration, stability, penetration, accumulation, environmental behavior, and intended physical consequence.

Whether the spell later hits, misses, is resisted, expires, or is wasted does not refund that cost.

Conceptually:

```text
DETERMINATION
"What should this spell be capable of doing if it succeeds?"
        ↓
Up-front mana cost
        ↓
Magical construct exists
        ↓
Hit / interaction
        ↓
Magic Resistance
        ↓
Successful reification up to the determined capability
        ↓
Normal physical / elemental resistance
```

This keeps resource accounting simple and makes named spells useful as practiced Determinations rather than arbitrary real-time damage sliders.

## 2. Up-Front Cost, Temporary Conjuration, and Permanent Materialization

Ordinary spellcasting pays its cost **up front according to Determination**.

Examples:

```text
Water Illusion

Determination:
    Look, move, and sound believably like water.
    Minimal physical consequence.

Cost:
    Low
```

```text
Water Ball

Determination:
    Behave like a water projectile.
    Deliver moderate impact.
    Leave moderate water accumulation on successful contact.

Cost:
    Moderate, paid immediately
```

```text
Water Lance

Determination:
    Focused projectile shape.
    High penetration.
    Significant force and water accumulation/effect on contact.

Cost:
    High, paid immediately
```

If the Water Lance misses, the caster still spent the mana required to create a construct capable of accomplishing that task. Cost follows **intent and capability**, not the amount of consequence that ultimately occurred.

### Temporary Conjuration

Most ordinary/legal magic creates **temporary magical constructs**, not permanent mundane matter.

A conjured rock is not secretly a real rock waiting for a commitment point. It is a magical construct determined to possess whatever rock-like properties its task requires:

```text
Conjured Boulder

Determination:
    rigid obstruction
    mass-like force
    collision
    blocking
    finite duration
```

It remains magical while it exists. It eventually dissipates when:

- its duration ends,
- sustaining mana is exhausted,
- it is destroyed,
- it is dismissed,
- or its defined task has succeeded/ended.

A temporary Stone Wall can therefore block movement or absorb an attack without ever becoming mundane geology.

A magical environmental construct may participate fully in ordinary world semantics—collision, visibility, force, temperature, obstruction, and similar interactions—while still remaining **magically sustained** and therefore temporary, dispellable, and dissipatable.

```text
Temporary Magical Stone Wall
    physically blocks
    still magical
    can expire / dissipate / be dispelled

Materialized Mundane Stone Wall
    physically blocks
    no longer magical
    remains after dispel
```

World interaction does **not** imply permanent materialization.


### Permanent Materialization

Making a conjuration **fully real and permanent from the start** is possible in principle, but it is catastrophically expensive.

The normal magical doctrine can simply be:

> **Permanent conjuration is too costly and dangerous for ordinary practice. It is not normally taught and is effectively prohibited. Only exceptional archmage-level practitioners could reliably perform it.**

Examples:

```text
Stone Shield
    temporary magical construct
    reasonable initial cost
    optional upkeep
    eventually dissipates

Create Permanent Stone Block
    actual mundane stone remains
    extreme materialization cost
    restricted / effectively banned
```

This keeps permanent matter creation as exceptional world lore rather than forcing ordinary spells to track configurable reification loci or turning mages into trivial industrial matter printers.

## 3. Reification as a General Property

Reification should not only mean "turn magical matter into real matter."

It is broader:

> **Reification is the successful physical realization of some property the magical construct was determined to be capable of producing.**

The caster already paid for that capability when the spell was created. Reification is the later consequence when the spell successfully interacts with reality.

Examples:

```text
Water Ball
    Determination:
        impact + water accumulation

    Reification:
        real momentum / force
        actual water / wetness where required

Fireball
    Determination:
        blunt impact + thermal consequence

    Reification:
        real force
        real heat / burns / ignition

Lightning
    Determination:
        electrical discharge

    Reification:
        actual charge/current
        secondary heating

Ice
    Determination:
        rigid cold material behavior

    World Interaction:
        temporary magical ice can block, chill, crack, shatter, and create slippery surfaces

    Reification:
        real thermal consequence where required
        mundane persistent ice only through exceptional deliberate materialization

Mana Blast
    Determination:
        forceful shockwave

    Reification:
        physical impulse / pressure
```

A spell does **not** need to become wholly mundane in order to have real consequences. Only the properties required by its Determination need to reify.

For normal spellcasting, there is no need to expose a detailed configurable "reification locus." The usual rule is simply that the construct remains magical and produces its determined real consequences when it successfully interacts with its target or environment.

## 4. Illusions

Illusions fall naturally out of the same system.

An illusion uses a **Determination focused on believable presentation rather than consequential physical effect**.

It still pays the minimum amount of mana and reification necessary to maintain believability:

- visible light,
- sound,
- minor air disturbance,
- trace warmth where appropriate,
- other small physical cues required for the illusion to hold together perceptually.

However, it deliberately avoids meaningful consequential reification such as:

- damaging heat,
- impact force,
- ignition,
- penetration,
- persistent material accumulation.

Example:

```text
Illusionary Fireball

Determination:
    look like a Fireball
    sound like a Fireball
    provide enough light / warmth / motion cues to be believable

Consequential Reification:
    none
```

This avoids requiring a separate magical perception channel. A camera may record the illusion because enough real light exists to create the image. A microphone may capture it because minimal sound pressure exists.

The important distinction is that the illusion does **not** pay to create the harmful or materially significant consequences of the represented phenomenon.

A skilled illusionist may spend more mana reproducing secondary cues accurately, making the illusion more convincing without turning it into a damaging spell.

Therefore:

> **An illusion is a conjuration determined to maximize believability while minimizing consequential reification.**

## 5. What Is an Element?

The word **Element** is intentionally a broad magical catch-all rather than a scientific category.

It does not require Earth, Heat, Water, Fire, Lightning, Ice, and similar phenomena to be the same kind of thing in mundane physics.

A useful working definition is:

> **An Element is an understood magical reality-template whose successful reification has an intrinsic effect outside of itself.**

A practical test is:

> **Does making this phenomenon real immediately create a physical interaction with the surrounding world?**

If yes, it is a strong candidate for elemental treatment.

Examples:

- Heat transfers thermal energy.
- Cool removes thermal energy.
- Water wets, displaces, immerses, and flows.
- Air or wind exerts pressure and carries motion.
- Fire transfers heat and can ignite suitable material.
- Lightning transfers charge/current and secondary heat.
- Ice imposes cold, rigidity, mass, and contact behavior.

By contrast, a conjured sword does not intrinsically "sword" anything merely by existing. It must be wielded, accelerated, angled, or otherwise used.

This is deliberately a semantic/gameplay definition. "Element" is the shared magical term for phenomena with immediate outward interaction, not an attempt to force them into one scientific class.

## 6. Elemental Relationships Without a Rigid Root Taxonomy

Earlier exploration divided magical primitives into categories such as **Substance/Form**, **Energy/State**, and **Force/Dynamics**. That distinction is no longer necessary and should not be treated as implementation ontology.

Likewise:

- Earth should not literally mean "solid phase."
- Water should not literally mean "liquid phase."
- Air should not literally mean "gas phase."

Those comparisons were useful while searching for the model, but the current definition of Element makes them unnecessary.

Relationships between phenomena should therefore remain descriptive rather than algebraically rigid:

```text
Ice
    derived from Water plus cooling / thermal removal

Steam
    derived from Water plus sufficient Heat

Lava
    heated / molten Earth or stone

Dust
    fragmented Earth dispersed or carried by Air

Fire
    Heat interacting with suitable material and environment
```

These relationships can later inform spell prerequisites, crafting, environmental reactions, skill progression, or lore without forcing every phenomenon into a literal `A + B = C` implementation.

## 7. Derived Elements and Phenomena

Familiar fantasy "elements" do not all need to be foundational.

They can be advanced combinations or derived manifestations.

| Derived phenomenon | Approximate composition / idea |
|---|---|
| **Ice** | Water + Cool |
| **Steam** | Water + Heat |
| **Lava** | Earth + Heat |
| **Dust** | Earth + Air / particulate form |
| **Fire** | Heat interacting with fuel/matter and usually Air |
| **Lightning** | Attract/Repel imbalance resolving through a medium |
| **Magnetism** | Attract/Repel applied to suitable matter |
| **Nature / Life** | Highly complex balance of Earth + Water + Air + Energy |

### Fire

Fire should probably **not** simply be `Heat + Heat`.

Doubling Heat should primarily mean *more heat*.

Fire is more interesting as a sustained energetic interaction between Heat, matter/fuel, and usually Air. This creates useful physical rules:

- heat stone enough and it glows or melts,
- heat wood in air and it can ignite,
- remove air and ordinary combustion stops,
- magical fire may initially imitate combustion before real ignition occurs.

### Metal

Metal is better treated as an advanced/refined form of Earth rather than `Earth + Attract`, because not all metals are magnetic.

Magnetized metal can then be:

```text
Metal + Attract/Repel
```

rather than forcing magnetism into the definition of all metal.

### Nature / Life

Nature or Life works well as a **very advanced result of balanced fundamentals**, not a primitive "green element."

Life is difficult because it is extraordinarily complex and constantly demands deep physical correctness.

A wolf-shaped magical construct can run, bite, and obey orders without actually being alive. Making it a real wolf is vastly harder because reality continually asks for:

- cells,
- blood,
- nerves,
- metabolism,
- organs,
- digestion,
- healing,
- memory,
- reproduction,
- microscopic chemistry,
- countless interacting biological processes.

Thus Life may be theoretically describable through fundamental domains while still being practically impossible to fully reify.

## 8. Complexity and the "Beaver Test"

A useful thought experiment for deciding whether a conjuration can remain abstract is:

> **How deeply does reality need to inspect it?**

A conjured wooden club does not need to contain literal cellulose fibers to strike someone like wood. Magic only needs to supply enough mass, rigidity, elasticity, and shape.

But if a beaver begins chewing the club, reality begins asking deeper questions:

- Does it splinter?
- What are those splinters made of?
- Are they digestible?
- Does it contain cellulose?
- Can it burn and chemically decompose?

At some point, the construct either has to reify into actual wood or fail to satisfy the interaction.

This gives a practical reason why simple elements and simple materials are easier to conjure than complex structures.

## 9. Determined Consequence Magnitude

The magnitude of intended consequence belongs to the spell's **Determination and up-front cost**. Reification is what later succeeds in becoming physically consequential.

For a Fireball-like construct:

| Intended consequence | Result |
|---|---|
| **Illusory** | Believable presentation; no meaningful consequential reification |
| **Trace** | Warmth or tiny physical effects |
| **Ignition** | Enough real heat to ignite susceptible material |
| **Damage** | Significant intentional thermal transfer |
| **Heavy Damage** | Severe burns, melting, major thermal output |
| **Full Materialization** | Exceptional/forbidden case: the phenomenon itself is deliberately made mundane and persistent |

This means **Fire Damage is intentional thermal reification already paid for by the Determination**.

A giant terrifying illusion may therefore cost less than a tiny magical ember determined to carry enough real heat to burn through steel.

Appearance and consequential power are separate variables.

## 10. Spell Families and Named Evolution

Named spells should be treated as **curated presets over a shared magical form**, not completely unrelated abilities.

The player chooses practiced intent rather than manually holding a button to select an arbitrary power magnitude.

Example Fire projectile family:

| Spell | Form / delivery | Reification | Physical semantic |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Fireball** | Ball projectile | Low | Blunt / impact |
| **Greater Fireball / Fire Orb** | Larger ball projectile | Higher | Blunt / impact |
| **Fire Spear** | Narrow penetrating projectile | Medium | Pierce |
| **Fire Javelin** | Larger / heavier spear form | Higher | Pierce |
| **Fire Blast** | Short forceful projection | Medium | Blunt + Concussive |
| **Explosion** | Radial forceful release | High | Blunt + Concussive + Radial |
| **Meteor** | Descending fire projectile | Very High | Impact + thermal; different trajectory |

A new spell name should correspond to a **semantic change the player can understand**.

- If only magnitude changes: `Greater Fireball` is acceptable.
- If shape changes: `Fire Spear -> Fire Javelin`.
- If propagation changes: `Fire Blast -> Explosion`.
- If trajectory changes: `Fireball -> Meteor`.
- If persistence changes: `Flame -> Fire Wall -> Inferno`.

## 11. Core Spell Composition

A useful conceptual spell structure is:

```text
Spell =
    Source / Element
    + Form
    + Delivery / Propagation
    + Determination
    + Presentation
```

The **Determination** defines the spell's intended capability and therefore its up-front mana cost.

That may include:

- expected impact magnitude,
- penetration,
- elemental consequence,
- area,
- duration,
- persistence,
- accumulation,
- environmental behavior,
- and how much consequential reification the construct is designed to support.

Examples:

```text
Fire Spear
    Source = Fire
    Form = Spear
    Delivery = Projectile

    Determination:
        focused penetration
        moderate-high thermal consequence
        Pierce + Heat

Fire Blast
    Source = Fire
    Form = Blast
    Delivery = Concussive

    Determination:
        short-range shockwave
        moderate thermal consequence
        Blunt + Heat
```

The **Form** determines how the spell physically interacts.

The **Determination** defines what it is meant to accomplish and what the caster pays for.

**Reification** is what later succeeds in becoming real when the spell interacts with the world.

The **Element** determines additional elemental behaviors and environmental consequences.

## 12. Pure Mana Magic

The same spell forms should exist without an elemental template attached.

Examples:

- Mana Ball
- Mana Orb
- Mana Spear
- Mana Javelin
- Mana Blast
- Mana Wall
- Mana Explosion

Pure Mana magic is not "reifying damage" in the abstract. It is better understood as **reifying force, shape, momentum, pressure, or other direct physical effects without also having to instantiate an elemental phenomenon**.

Examples:

```text
Mana Ball
    Form = Ball
    Physical = Blunt
    Element = None

Mana Spear
    Form = Spear
    Physical = Pierce
    Element = None

Mana Blast
    Form = Blast
    Physical = Blunt
    Propagation = Concussive
    Element = None
```

Pure Mana therefore has a natural niche:

| Pure Mana | Elemental Magic |
|---|---|
| More of the magical budget can go directly into force/form/reification | Pays for elemental behavior too |
| No elemental resistance check | Subject to relevant elemental resistance |
| Usually immediate/direct | Can create persistent or secondary effects |
| Fewer environmental interactions | Rich environmental interactions |
| Mostly force/physical semantics | Physical + elemental semantics |
| Primarily countered by Magic Resistance | Countered by Magic Resistance plus relevant mundane resistance |

A Mana Spear may therefore penetrate better than an equal-cost Fire Spear, because it is not also paying to create Heat, flame behavior, ignition, etc.

The Fire Spear, however, can pierce, burn, ignite clothing, start fires, and possibly leave persistent heat effects.

---

### Pure Mana Is Direct, Not Universally Superior

Pure Mana should not automatically have the best damage-per-mana.

Its advantages are:

- directness,
- predictable force,
- flexible shaping,
- lack of elemental baggage,
- fewer specialized elemental resistances.

Elemental magic can outperform Pure Mana when the environment amplifies the result.

A modest Fire spell that ignites dry grass, oil, timber, or another combustible environment may produce far more total consequence than an equal-cost Mana Blast because mundane combustion continues after the original spell has done its job.

Pure Mana is therefore the cleanest **direct-force** option, not the universally most efficient source of damage.

## 13. Physical Damage Semantics

The basic physical damage language should stay small.

### Primary Physical Damage Types

| Damage type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Slash** | Force distributed along an edge | Sword cuts, axes, whips, claws |
| **Pierce** | Force concentrated into a point | Arrows, spears, thrusting swords, javelins, spikes |
| **Blunt** | Force delivered through a surface | Maces, hammers, fists, kicks, rocks |

Most weapons can be described with those three.

A weapon can support more than one damage capability:

```text
Spiked Mace
    Blunt  = primary
    Pierce = secondary

Sword Slash
    Slash

Sword Thrust
    Pierce

Pommel Strike
    Blunt
```

A weapon does not necessarily own one immutable damage type. **The attack determines which capability is being used.**

---

## 14. Concussive as Propagation, Not a Fourth Damage Type

Concussive is extremely close to Blunt, so it is more useful as a **delivery / propagation property** than as a peer damage type.

> **Concussive = blunt-like force propagated as a pressure or shock wave rather than through ordinary direct physical contact.**

Examples:

```text
Mace
    Damage = Blunt
    Propagation = Contact

Fire Blast
    Damage = Blunt + Heat
    Propagation = Concussive

Explosion
    Damage = Blunt + Heat
    Propagation = Concussive + Radial

Frost Nova
    Damage = Blunt + Cold
    Propagation = Concussive + Radial
```

Concussive force can potentially:

- spread outward,
- hit multiple targets,
- travel around or through openings,
- continue beyond an initial surface,
- fall off with distance,
- shove or stagger,
- transmit through some materials.

This gives the system richer behavior without inventing an unnecessary fourth physical damage category.

## 15. Resistance Model

### Magic Resistance

**Within Wizardry, Magic Resistance is reification rejection.**

Within Wizardry it answers:

> **Can this magical construct successfully make its intended effect real against me?**

It is checked before an incoming magical effect fully commits to reality.

A sufficiently high Magic Resistance can potentially nullify a magical attack completely.

### Mundane / Elemental Resistance

Once an effect successfully reifies, normal physical or biological resistance applies.

Examples:

```text
Fireball
    Magic Resistance
        ↓
    surviving Heat + impact become real
        ↓
    Heat Resistance / armor / physical mitigation
        ↓
    final consequence
```

But standing in a real bonfire has no magical stage:

```text
Real Fire
    ↓
Heat Resistance
    ↓
final consequence
```

This means a player's ordinary Fire/Heat Resistance still behaves the way players expect against both magical and mundane heat.

The same applies to poison.

### Poison Example

```text
Snake Venom
    real toxin
        ↓
    Poison Resistance

Nightshade
    real toxin
        ↓
    Poison Resistance

Magical Poison Cloud
    Magic Resistance
        ↓
    whatever toxicity successfully reifies
        ↓
    Poison Resistance
```

This supports natural tolerance, acquired immunity, species-specific resistance, and magical resistance without conflating them.

### Important Rule

> **Magic Resistance resists the spell's attempt to enter reality.  
> Specific resistance determines how well the target handles what successfully became real.**

### Resistance vs. Dispel

**Magic Resistance** and **Dispel** are different operations.

```text
Magic Resistance
    "Can this incoming Wizardry effect successfully impose
     its determined consequence on me?"

Dispel
    "Can I destabilize, terminate, or unravel
     an existing magical construct?"
```

Magic Resistance is primarily about resisting an effect **against the target**.

Dispel is about attacking the continued magical existence of:

- walls,
- summons,
- illusions,
- fields,
- wards,
- sustained constructs,
- other ongoing magical effects.

A magical Stone Wall can physically block someone regardless of their personal Magic Resistance, but it may still be vulnerable to Dispel because it remains a magical construct.

Dispel can later have its own contest rules—caster skill, construct stability, tier, remaining duration, reinforcement, or other factors—without conflating it with personal resistance.

### Other Traditions

The above mechanism should **not** automatically be applied to every form of magic.

- Wizardry is resisted through reification rejection.
- Chivalric/Holy effects may deliberately ignore ordinary resistance and instead be limited by scarce Piety/Grace.
- Druidic effects may often create ordinary environmental consequences that are resisted only through mundane simulation.
- Blood Magic may rely on sympathetic links, sacrifice, or actual blood as substrate rather than Wizardry-style reification.
- Dark/Black Magic may use shadows, corpses, negative space, or outer-realm mechanics.
- Malefic/Infernal magic may have its own counters or simply be dangerous precisely because conventional defenses do not map cleanly onto it.

A shared UI stat called `Magic Resistance` could still exist later if useful, but the lore/mechanism should not be forced to be identical across traditions.

## 16. Mixed Spell Consequences

A spell can have several separately meaningful consequences.

Example:

```text
Fire Spear
    Pierce
    Heat

    Force Reification
    Thermal Reification
```

A target may reject some of the spell through Magic Resistance.

Whatever survives may then be separately handled by:

- armor / physical mitigation against Pierce,
- Heat Resistance against thermal injury.

Likewise:

```text
Fire Blast
    Heat Reification
    Force Reification
    Propagation = Concussive
```

A heat-resistant creature may still be thrown backward by the shockwave.

This is preferable to assigning every spell one arbitrary "damage color."

## 17. Element vs. Conjuration vs. Environmental Effect

These should remain distinct concepts.

### Element

A reality-template whose successful reification produces an inherent physical interaction.

Examples:

- Heat
- Cool
- Water
- Air / pressure
- Electricity
- possibly advanced derived elements such as Fire or Ice

### Derived Element / Phenomenon

A combination of primitives that still has immediate physical behavior.

Examples:

- Ice
- Steam
- Lava
- Lightning
- Fire

### Conjuration

A magically sustained object, material, or structure whose consequences depend primarily on its form, placement, or later use.

Examples:

- conjured sword,
- wooden club,
- wall of stone,
- shaped platform,
- constructed barrier.

### Environmental Effect

An element or conjuration made to exist **in a region** rather than directly imposed on a target.

Examples:

- Fog,
- Dust Cloud,
- Sandstorm,
- Wall of Fire,
- Ice Floor,
- Pool of Water,
- Stone Wall.

This distinction is important because not every magical obstacle should be something a target can simply "resist."

---

## 18. Direct Reification vs. Environmental Reification

There are at least two major interaction models.

### Targeted / Contact Reification

The spell is trying to make an effect real **against a target**.

Examples:

- Fireball
- Lightning
- Ice Spear
- Mana Spear
- Poison Bolt

Magic Resistance is relevant because the spell is trying to impose reality on or against the target.

### Environmental Reification

The spell makes something exist **in a place**.

Examples:

- Fog
- Dust Cloud
- Sandstorm
- Stone Wall
- Wall of Fire
- Pool of Water
- Ice Floor

Once the environment exists, creatures interact with it through normal simulation.

A character with high Magic Resistance should not automatically be able to see through magical fog if the fog has already been successfully created in the environment.

The spell is not applying a `Blind` debuff to the character. There is physically obscuring matter or magical structure between the observer and the target.

Likewise, a Stone Wall does not apply a `Blocked` status effect. There is simply a wall.

---

## 19. Avoid Status Effects When Simulation Already Produces the Result

A general rule:

> **Do not create a status effect when the simulated phenomenon already explains the consequence.**

A Dust spell may create:

```text
Environmental:
    reduced visibility
    particulate obstruction
    projectile interference
    wind / motion

Target Interaction:
    eye irritation
    abrasion
    breathing interference
```

The visibility loss does not need a `Blind` debuff if the world is genuinely obscured.

However, actual particles entering someone's eyes may create a legitimate temporary irritation/stun effect.

This allows environmental magic to feel physical rather than merely being a collection of hidden status tags.

---

## 20. Dust as a Borderline Example

Dust is useful specifically because it demonstrates that something does **not** need to be a fundamental element to support a full spell family.

Dust is probably:

```text
Earth + Air + particulate form
```

rather than a root element.

It could still support:

```text
Dust
    brief burst
    temporary vision disruption / irritation

Dust Cloud
    persistent local obstruction

Sandstorm
    large sustained moving field
    heavy vision obstruction
    possible abrasion / force / projectile interference
```

A Sandstorm's main visibility effect is environmental rather than something directly "resisted" by the target.

This distinction should be preserved.

## 21. Magic Skill and Efficiency

A master's advantage should not only be:

> "My Fireball costs 8 mana instead of 10."

A deeper expression of mastery is:

> **A novice overpays for a poorly constrained Determination. A master pays for exactly the capability required.**

A novice may waste magic by:

- creating excess force,
- overbuilding duration,
- over-reifying matter or heat,
- producing unnecessary stability,
- using an inefficient form for the intended task.

A master understands:

- which properties actually need to be present,
- how much consequence the spell really needs,
- how little persistence is necessary,
- how to focus force into useful geometries,
- how to choose efficient environmental rather than direct-target solutions,
- how to make the Determination precise enough that little mana is wasted.

This makes magical efficiency part of the world's metaphysics rather than merely a numeric discount.

## 22. Current Working Vocabulary

### Fundamental Terms

**Magic**  
A system capable of creating temporary reality-emulating constructs and selectively committing their intended consequences into ordinary reality.

**Conjuration**  
The magical construct or imitation itself.

**Determination**  
The spell's declared intent/capability: what the construct is being created and paid up front to be able to accomplish.

**Reification**  
The successful physical realization of some determined property of a magical construct.

**Materialization**  
Deliberately turning most or all of a conjuration into persistent mundane matter or energy. Extremely expensive and normally restricted or untaught.

**Persistence**  
The point at which a result exists without the original magical construct needing to sustain it.

**Illusion**  
A conjuration determined to maximize believable presentation while minimizing consequential reification.

**Element**  
A broad magical category for an understood reality-template whose successful reification intrinsically interacts with the surrounding world.

**Derived Element / Phenomenon**  
An advanced or combined elemental phenomenon that still possesses immediate physical behavior.

**Conjured Structure / Object**  
A temporary magical construct whose consequences depend on geometry, use, placement, or further action.

**Environmental Effect**  
A phenomenon made to exist in a region rather than directly imposed on a target.

**Magic Resistance**  
General resistance to magical reification.

**Physical / Elemental Resistance**  
Resistance to the real phenomenon after it has successfully reified, or when that phenomenon originates mundanely.

### Physical Damage

- Slash
- Pierce
- Blunt

### Propagation / Delivery Examples

- Contact
- Projectile
- Concussive
- Radial
- Beam
- Persistent / Field

### Example Spell Structure

```text
Spell
    Source / Element
    Form
    Delivery / Propagation
    Determination
    Presentation

Determination
    intended magnitude
    penetration / force
    elemental consequence
    duration / area / persistence
    expected consequential reification

Result
    successful reification
    physical damage semantics
    elemental consequences
    environmental consequences
```

## 23. Guiding Principles

1. **Mana cost is paid up front according to Determination: what the spell is intended to be capable of doing.**
2. **A miss, resistance, expiration, or wasted spell does not refund its cost.**
3. **Ordinary conjurations are temporary magical constructs; they do not normally become permanent mundane matter.**
4. **Permanent materialization is catastrophically expensive, effectively restricted, and generally outside ordinary taught magic.**
5. **Reification is the successful physical realization of a determined magical property.**
6. **Illusions pay only for enough physical presentation to remain believable and avoid consequential reification.**
7. **Element is a broad magical term for phenomena whose reification intrinsically affects the surrounding world.**
8. **Do not force elements into rigid scientific root categories unless a later mechanic actually requires it.**
9. **Not every spell family needs to be an element.**
10. **Spell names should represent meaningful changes in form, propagation, magnitude, or persistence.**
11. **Pure Mana and elemental magic should share the same spell-form language.**
12. **Slash, Pierce, and Blunt cover the basic physical damage model; Concussive is primarily a propagation behavior.**
13. **Magic Resistance rejects reification; mundane resistance handles what successfully becomes real.**
14. **Environmental effects should be simulated as environment whenever possible rather than reduced to status effects.**
15. **Mastery should improve the precision and efficiency of Determination, not merely apply an arbitrary mana discount.**
16. **The system should remain compositional: Element + Form + Delivery + Determination should explain most spells without bespoke exceptions.**

---

## 24. Multiple Magical Traditions

The world should not force every supernatural effect through Wizardry's rules.

A better model is that **different magical traditions have different foundational principles**. They may sometimes accomplish similar player-facing results, but they do not necessarily achieve them in the same way.

A useful conceptual separation is:

```text
TRADITION / PRINCIPLE
    Wizardry
    Dark / Black Magic
    Druidism
    Blood Magic
    Chivalry / Holy Magic
    Malefic / Infernal Magic
    ...

DISCIPLINE / EFFECT
    Elemental
    Summoning
    Healing
    Binding
    Necromancy
    Divination
    Transformation
    Enchantment
    ...

METHOD
    Immediate Cast
    Channeled
    Ritual
    Prayer
    Sacrifice
    Inscription
    Prepared Circle
    ...
```

Not every combination needs to exist. If `Blood Ritual Summoning` has no compelling semantic reason to exist, it does not need a filler spell merely to complete a matrix.

Likewise, **Ritual** is better treated primarily as a casting method than as one universal school. Different traditions can use rituals to exceed their normal operating limits in tradition-specific ways.

---

## 25. Wizardry / Arcane / Elemental Magic

Wizardry is the tradition most completely described by the earlier sections of this document.

Its core principle is:

> **Construct a magical approximation according to a Determination, then allow the intended consequences to reify when the construct successfully interacts with reality.**

This includes Mana Ball / Mana Spear / Mana Blast, elemental projectiles, walls and fields, illusions, temporary conjured objects, and most of the Element + Form + Delivery + Determination spell language.

### Wizardry Summoning

Ordinary Wizardry summoning need not create life. A wizard can construct something that looks alive, moves alive, attacks like a living thing, follows instructions, and reacts believably while still being only a sophisticated magical construct.

A summoned wolf can therefore be a very convincing **wolf-shaped Determination**, not a living wolf. When destroyed or expired, it dissipates.

### Wizardry Ritual Summoning

Ritual preparation can allow Wizardry to exceed ordinary conjuration and establish a real connection to an entity that already exists elsewhere. The ritual is not building a fake creature; it is creating a controlled bridge or invitation that allows a genuine external entity to arrive.

The exact entity families should remain tradition-sensitive. Demonic summoning may be better reserved for explicitly Malefic/Infernal traditions, while Wizardry ritual summoning can remain the broader controlled technique for external entities.

---

## 26. Dark / Black Magic and Necromancy

Dark or Black Magic should remain **distinct from genuinely evil or demonic magic**. It is dangerous, socially suspicious, and death-adjacent, but it is not inherently unholy, chaotic, or evil.

A useful origin is that it began as a **twisted, incomplete, or aggressively experimental descendant of Wizardry**. Early practitioners may have been failed or unconventional wizards, researchers trying to push Wizardry beyond its limits, experimenters attempting resurrection or true life creation, or practitioners exploring shadows, absence, outer realms, and negative space.

Some of their "failures" turned out to be stable and useful enough to become a mature tradition of their own.

Its core principle is roughly:

> **Use absence, death, shadow, negative space, outer-realm adjacency, and existing dead structure to avoid constructing the complete thing Wizardry would normally require.**

### Environmental Strength

If Dark Magic literally manipulates shadow and negative space, environmental darkness matters.

```text
Bright noon
    fewer existing shadows / more work to deepen or create them
    lower efficiency

Interiors / forests / caves
    abundant shadow
    normal efficiency

Night / deep underground
    darkness is everywhere
    highest efficiency
```

The caster is not arbitrarily receiving a "night bonus." There is simply more usable substrate already present.

### Necromancy as a Discipline

Necromancy can sit inside the broader Dark/Black tradition.

`Raise Dead` is not resurrection. It may have originated in experiments attempting to restore life, but the eventual discovery was:

> **The dead cannot be restored to true life through this technique, but their remaining structure can be used as a magical scaffold.**

```text
Raise Skeleton
    existing bones supply structure
    magic supplies motion, coordination, intent, and persistence

Raise Corpse
    corpse supplies more of the original physical structure
    magic substitutes for metabolism, nervous control, and life
```

This is cheaper than fully conjuring a convincing humanoid construct from nothing because much of the required structure already exists.

### Negative / Shadow Constructs

Dark Magic can also create incomplete, negative approximations: Shadow Steeds / Nightmares, Shadow Hounds, Shades, Wraith-like forms, black or hollow weapons, and negative silhouettes.

A Wizard may construct a convincing horse. A Dark Mage may only need:

> **"Something horse-shaped enough to carry me."**

The result can be obviously incomplete, shadow-like, cold, hollow, skeletal, or smoke-edged because that is what the technique actually produces.

### Dark Ritual Summoning

Dark Ritual Summoning should not automatically mean "summon demon." Its advanced summons can instead be Nightmares, shadow creatures, death creatures, negative-reality entities, outer-realm horrors, or Lovecraftian/eldritch beings that do not fit ordinary reality cleanly.

The ritual gives enough structure or opening for something from a death/shadow/negative realm to manifest.

---

## 27. Dark Magic and the Discovery of Teleportation

Dark Magic is a natural candidate for being the **first tradition to discover teleportation**.

Its practitioners were already experimenting with outer realms, negative space, putting things partly outside ordinary reality, pulling things into reality, and moving through shadow-adjacent spaces.

Eventually someone realized:

> **Instead of pulling something else through the gap, use the gap as a bridge from here to there.**

Early forms might have been:

```text
Shadow Step
    enter one shadow
    emerge from another nearby shadow

Shadow Walk
    travel partly through negative / outer space

Teleportation
    leave normal space
    traverse a short non-Euclidean path
    re-enter elsewhere
```

Wizardry later observes the technique, formalizes the underlying spatial trick, and produces a safer Wizardry-compatible implementation.

This creates legitimate historical bad blood:

> **"You call us failed wizards, but you learned teleportation from us."**

Wizardry may genuinely have made teleportation safer, more precise, and more teachable. Dark Magic may genuinely have discovered it first.

That rivalry can recur elsewhere: Dark Magic finds strange possibilities by pushing into dangerous edges of reality; Wizardry later systematizes and sanitizes the technique.

---

## 28. Druidic / Nature Magic

Druidic magic should not feel like Wizardry with green particles.

Its core principle is closer to:

> **Influence, accelerate, attract, awaken, or cooperate with natural systems that already exist.**

Creating a fully mature oak from empty stone should be difficult or impossible. Making existing roots grow rapidly may be comparatively easy.

### Druidic Summoning

Ordinary Druidic summoning can be **attraction rather than creation** through pheromone-like influence, supernatural scent, animal calls, instinct manipulation, or ecological signaling.

```text
Call Wolves
    nearby wolves become strongly inclined to approach

Vermin Call
    nearby rats / insects / similar creatures converge

Dungeon Call
    nearby monsters may begin moving toward the caster
```

Importantly, a call does not necessarily make the creature friendly. Calling in or near a dungeon may be extremely dangerous because the caster is effectively announcing themselves to the local ecosystem.

### Druidic Ritual Summoning

Ritual Druidism can go deeper and awaken/call entities that require stronger environmental participation: Elementals, Ents, Dryads, ancient beasts, or local nature spirits.

These may require suitable surroundings rather than appearing anywhere on demand.

---

## 29. Blood Magic

Blood Magic should have its own compact set of principles rather than borrowing Wizardry's entire rule system.

Three strong foundations are:

### Blood as Substrate

Actual blood can be shaped into temporary weapons or defenses: Blood Dagger, Blood Spear, Blood Shield, Blood Armor.

A useful equipment rule is:

```text
Blood Weapon / Shield
    finite durability
    cannot be repaired conventionally
    durability reaches 0
        -> construct breaks / collapses
```

It may be possible to reinforce the item with more blood, but it is not a normal blacksmith-repairable object.

### Blood as Sympathetic Identity

Blood provides a metaphysical link to its source. That enables locating the owner, tracking the owner, targeting the owner at range, curses, links, species/kin attraction, and sympathetic transfer.

### Blood as Exchange

Blood and vitality can also be used as a resource-transfer system: Life Leech, sacrifice health for stronger effects, transfer wounds, share vitality, or redirect life through an established link.

### Blood Summoning

If the caster possesses the blood of a creature or person, it can serve as a sympathetic reference.

A normal Blood Summoning effect might simply influence the **closest matching living source** to approach. That makes the identity/search portion nearly free because the blood already supplies the link.

A possible `Blood Ritual Summoning` could forcibly relocate the linked being, but this is not required. If the concept is not compelling enough, the combination can simply remain unsupported.

---

## 30. Chivalry / Holy Magic

Chivalry should be unusually powerful but intentionally awkward to sustain.

It does not use ordinary Mana as its primary spell resource. Instead it uses something like **Piety**, **Grace**, or **Favor**; the exact final name can be chosen later.

Its balancing principle is:

> **Chivalric effects are exceptionally strong and reliable because their resource is slow, behavioral, and inconvenient to replenish.**

### Piety / Grace Acquisition

Piety is gained through meaningful practice rather than passive regeneration: prayer, donations, service, protecting others, healing others, fulfilling vows, charitable deeds, holy quests, and time at shrines or temples.

Prayer may regenerate it slowly, perhaps more effectively at consecrated locations.

A player can therefore run out of Piety in the middle of a dungeon and be unable to simply drink a potion or wait thirty seconds for it to return. They may have to leave, pray, serve, donate, or perform meaningful deeds before they are fully capable again.

### Chivalric Power Level

Because the resource is awkward to rebuild, Chivalric abilities can be significantly stronger than ordinary equivalents.

Examples include exceptionally powerful healing, reliable poison cleansing, `Clear Curse` as an expensive but extremely effective curse-removal technique, powerful wards, Holy/Chivalric direct damage, and possibly resurrection at the extreme high end if resurrection exists anywhere at all.

`Holy Damage` may bypass ordinary resistance if that proves healthy in playtesting, but this should remain **provisional rather than axiomatic**. It is balanced by scarcity and commitment rather than by passing through multiple resistance layers.

### Piety vs. Mastery

Current resource and permanent progression should be separate:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Piety / Grace** | Consumable resource used to invoke Chivalric powers |
| **Devotion / Chivalry Skill** | Permanent mastery of the tradition |
| **Disciplines / Rites** | Abilities unlocked through mastery |
| **Vows / Practices** | Activities that generate resource and may advance mastery |

A character can learn basic Chivalry, use a few spells, then effectively abandon the discipline. They may still gain an occasional point of Piety from random good deeds and eventually be able to cast something again, but they will remain weak and underdeveloped because they are not actively living the practice.

### Chivalry as a Lifelong Commitment

The world-lore principle should be something like:

> **Using Chivalry is not a hobby or a job. It is a lifelong commitment.**

A casual dabbler may know a Heal rite but almost never have enough Piety to use it.

A dedicated Paladin prays regularly, performs service, gives offerings, fulfills vows, protects others, deliberately maintains a reserve of Grace, and trains toward higher disciplines.

High-level Chivalric abilities may require more than skill points. They can require behavioral or service milestones.

```text
Restoration
    Chivalry mastery requirement
    heal/protect others repeatedly
    complete service rite

Clear Curse
    higher mastery
    purification discipline
    consecration rite

Holy Judgment
    very high mastery
    major fulfilled oath
```

A useful world phrase is:

> **Anyone can learn a prayer. A Paladin is the person still saying it twenty years later.**

---

## 31. Malefic / Infernal / Profane Magic

There should be a magical tradition that is **genuinely malignant**, distinct from Dark/Black Magic.

Dark Magic is creepy, death-adjacent, shadow-based, and socially suspicious. Malefic/Infernal/Profane Magic is different because its underlying principle is itself predatory or corruptive.

A useful definition is:

> **Malefic Magic derives power from violation, suffering, corruption, predation, destruction, or hostile external entities.**

The final name can be chosen later.

### Acquisition

This should not be a normal trainer-taught school.

A fitting acquisition method is an extremely rare reward from a deliberately evil, bloodthirsty, or twisted dungeon.

The implied feeling is:

> *You survived everything I put in front of you. You have impressed whatever intelligence or will is behind this place. Here is knowledge you should probably never have. Use it upon the world—or destroy yourself with it. Either result is acceptable.*

This makes the dungeon feel less like a treasure vault and more like a **recruitment/filter mechanism**.

### Example Abilities

Possible Malefic effects include:

- **Summon Demon**
- **Corpse Explosion**
- **Plague**
- **Vampirism**
- **Possession**
- **Desecrate**
- **Soul Burn**
- **Demonic Pact**
- **Blood Boil**
- **Raise Abomination**

These should feel socially and morally unacceptable for understandable reasons.

### Distinction from Similar Traditions

**Necromancy / Dark Magic:**

> "This corpse is a useful scaffold."

**Malefic Corpse Explosion:**

> "This corpse is fuel for a weapon."

**Blood Magic Life Leech:**

> establishes a blood/life connection and redirects some vitality from successful harm.

**Malefic Vampirism:**

> directly consumes another creature's vitality in order to replenish the caster.

The overlap in visuals should not erase the difference in underlying principle.

### Power and Consequences

Malefic spells may be unusually powerful but can carry ugly mechanical or social consequences: consuming corpses or living resources, corruption or contamination, attracting hostile entities, collateral damage, poor controllability, criminality, unmistakable forensic evidence, or effects that are difficult to heal or cleanse.

It should not merely be "Wizardry, but purple and stronger."

---

## 32. Ritual as a Method

**Ritual** should primarily describe a method of performing magic rather than one universal tradition.

A ritual trades time, preparation, materials, geometry, location, participants, and risk for access to effects that exceed the normal limits of the tradition.

### Wizardry Ritual

Can establish controlled connections or large, precise effects that ordinary temporary conjuration cannot easily support.

### Dark Ritual

Can open paths into shadow/death/negative realms or allow nightmare/eldritch entities to manifest.

### Druidic Ritual

Can awaken or call Elementals, Ents, Dryads, ancient beasts, or nature spirits through suitable environments.

### Blood Ritual

May support extreme sympathetic effects if a compelling mechanic emerges, but no filler spell is required.

### Chivalric Rite

Can consecrate, purify, clear powerful curses, establish major wards, or invoke other extremely costly devotional effects.

The core rule is:

> **Rituals let a tradition temporarily exceed its ordinary operational envelope, but they still obey that tradition's identity.**

---

## 33. Tradition Signatures

The current traditions can be summarized as:

| Tradition | Foundational idea |
|---|---|
| **Wizardry** | Construct a magical approximation according to a Determination and reify its intended consequences |
| **Dark / Black Magic** | Exploit shadow, absence, death, negative space, outer realms, and incomplete structure |
| **Druidism** | Influence, accelerate, attract, awaken, or cooperate with existing natural systems |
| **Blood Magic** | Use blood as substrate, sympathetic identity, sacrifice, and exchange |
| **Chivalry / Holy Magic** | Spend slowly accumulated Piety/Grace to invoke unusually powerful, authoritative effects |
| **Malefic / Infernal Magic** | Gain power through corruption, predation, violation, suffering, or hostile external entities |
| **Ritual** | A cross-tradition method for exceeding normal limits through preparation and investment |

A useful design rule is:

> **Each tradition should have a small number of foundational rules that explain most of its magic.**

The goal is not to make every school capable of the same spell list. When two traditions both have something called `Summoning`, the player-facing goal may be similar while the underlying act is completely different.

---

## 34. Cross-System Integration

The magic model should plug into the rest of UltraSim without forcing the simulation core to understand spell lore.

| System | Relationship to Magic |
|---|---|
| **Skills** | Improve Determination precision, efficiency, stability, and access to stronger techniques |
| **Items / Augments** | Store, focus, modify, enable, or bias repeatable magical effects |
| **Environment** | Receives actual consequences such as force, fire, water, obstruction, visibility loss, poison, and temperature |
| **Simulation Core** | Consumes resulting world state and consequences rather than needing to understand the lore of the spell |
| **Combat / Resistance** | Resolves Slash/Pierce/Blunt, Heat, Poison, armor, knockback, and tradition-specific magical defenses |
| **Deterministic Fate** | Spell outcomes use authoritative deterministic state/RNG like the rest of the simulation |

A Fireball does not need to remain a special-case concept forever after impact.

Wizardry resolves the magical portion, then hands ordinary consequences to the world:

```text
Blunt / Impulse = X
Heat = Y
Ignition Attempt = Z
Environmental Change = ...
```

From that point onward, the simulation processes the resulting state normally.

## 35. Revised Open Questions / Future Angles

The current language is coherent enough to build on, but several areas can be explored later without changing the foundation:

- final naming for Dark/Black Magic,
- final naming for Malefic/Infernal/Profane Magic,
- final naming for Piety/Grace/Favor,
- whether Chivalric resurrection exists,
- how Holy damage interacts with absolute immunity or special bosses if it has no ordinary resistance,
- how specific Wizardry Determinations are learned, improved, and stored,
- whether casters can improvise Determinations or must work from practiced forms,
- how sustained maintenance contributes to mana cost,
- how temporary conjured objects behave when partially destroyed,
- whether permanent materialization produces backlash or legal consequences beyond mana cost,
- how mind magic, divination, enchantment, transformation, and healing map across traditions,
- whether Cool remains an Element in its own right or is treated as magical heat removal,
- how Attract/Repel or other force-oriented elements should be represented if they become useful,
- how spell interruption affects temporary conjurations and rituals,
- how Dispel contests construct stability, reinforcement, duration, and caster skill,
- whether permanent enchantments are sustained magic or alterations to reality itself,
- how much environmental state should be simulated versus abstracted for performance.

These should be resolved only when a mechanic actually requires the distinction.
